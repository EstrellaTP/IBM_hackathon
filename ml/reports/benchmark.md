# Latency benchmark on LinuxONE

Generated by `python -m tools.benchmark.run_benchmark`. Client: laptop (Madrid) over the internet. Each patient sends 10 s of synthetic wrist signal every 10 s (accelerometer at 4 Hz, HR 1 Hz, EDA 4 Hz) 
for 150 s per level.

- `detector_ms`: detection compute inside the detector container (IBM Z).
- `server_ms`: ingest-api from `ingested_at` to the response (detector call, encrypted storage, audit), all on LinuxONE.
- `client_ms`: round trip seen by the client (adds TLS and the network).

## Optimisation driven by the measurement (same benchmark, 1,000 patients)

| Version | server p50 | server p99 | ingest-api CPU |
|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline (`benchmark_baseline.md`) | 26.1 ms | 160.2 ms | 70 % of one core |
| + SQLite `synchronous=NORMAL` (hypothesis: disk sync) | 24.1 ms | 186.2 ms | — (no gain, reverted) |
| + batch serialised once (Rust encoder) | 21.3 ms | 153.8 ms | 58 % |
| + `httptools` and `uvloop` in every service (this report) | **16.9 ms** | **121.3 ms** | — |

## Interpretation

- **Inference is flat**: the detector's compute stays at 0.3 ms (p50) and below 1 ms
  (p99) from 1 to 1,000 concurrent patients.
- **Server time is low but not flat**: p50 stays at 6-17 ms; p99 grows to 121 ms at
  1,000 patients (100 batches per second) on this 3-vCPU guest. `docker stats` showed
  the single ingest-api process as the busiest component (JSON parsing and
  validation in one Python event loop), not the model and not the database.
- **Network vs server**: from Madrid the round trip adds 110-140 ms (p50), i.e. the
  transatlantic hop to the datathon VM, not LinuxONE.
- **Next step to flatten p99** (not done): run several ingest-api processes, each
  owning a shard of patients (the phone WebSockets live in process memory), on a
  LinuxONE partition with more cores.
- Even at the 99th percentile with 1,000 patients, the question is pushed about 0.1 s
  after the batch arrives: well within the episode it refers to.
